PROBLEM STATEMENT
PROBLEM
  • Students have limited practical exposure to defensive cybersecurity operations even though there are courses dedicated for it
  • There is a lack of an SOC lab that supports blue team operations
  • They waste time manually configuring environment and troubleshoot compatibility issues before starting exercises within those classes
IMPACT
  • Reduces the time available for meaningful practice -> leading to suboptimal learning outcomes
  • Studies show that scenario-based labs improve skill acquisition
ROOT CAUSE
  • Lack of an environment that teaches clear defensive workflow and toolchain practically
GAP
  • Such lab can fosters engagement, by allowing students to focus on the workflow rather than the setup, supporting deeper understanding of how detection, response, and case management are connected in a real defense environment
PROPOSED SOLUTION
  • A pre-configured Blue Team SOC lab environment with ready to use setup has integrated tools, and structured defensive workflows
EXPECTED OUTCOME
  • Enables student to have real-life, hands-on defensive activities
  • Improve understanding of SOC workflow, engagement and learning effectiveness
No Research Questions(s) Research Objectives(s) Methods Expected Outcome
1 What are the standard architectures, tools, and configurations required to design and build a blue team defense lab within a SOC lab with minimal resources using open source tools such as Wazuh, ClamAV, and TheHive?
  • To identify the tools and justify the selection of blue team tools (e.g., Wazuh agent/manager, ClamAV, TheHive) and their roles within a SOC architecture.
  • To develop a design for constructing a blue team defense lab.
  • To define the configurations, integrations, and communication flow between components (endpoint → SIEM → response platform).
 Literature review Have a validated blueprint that outlines:
  1. Role of tools and interactions (Wazuh + ClamAV + TheHive).
  2. A practical and deployable configuration suitable for low-resource environments.
  3. Flow of data between components.
2 How are the workflow and operational procedure carried out in real-world SOC environments for blue team activities particularly in relation to detection, incident analysis, and response coordination?
  • To understand real-world blue team workflow including alert triage, incident escalation, and response procedures.
  • Understand procedures followed by SOC analyst in the blue team and how they interact with SIEM and incident response platforms during malware-related incidents.
  • Incorporate the workflow and procedure from insights taken from interviews into the lab design, ensuring alignment with realistic blue team operations.
 Interview
  • Validation of whether the proposed system reflects real SOC practices.
  • Insights into improving lab realism, including handling multiple users, event correlation, and operational coordination.
3 How effective is the developed lab in terms of workflow and system integration with the existing infrastructure?
  • To test whether all the components (Wazuh, ClamAV, TheHive) set up function as intended cohesively across detection, analysis, and response stages.
  • Assess the efficiency and accuracy of the workflow, including alert generation, investigation, and containment actions.
  • Measure the lab’s effectiveness using performance metrics such as detection accuracy and response time.
 Experiment
  • Address all inconsistencies, errors. The feedback will be used to fix and output a working system.
  • A fully functional and optimized blue team lab environment.
PROPOSED DESIGN
PROJECT SCOPE
Level Function Description
Basic System shall provide hardened VM templates VMs in lab will be installed with VM images that has been hardened based on CIS benchmarks
System will provide tools needed to carry out SOC lab operations The system will be equipped with Wazuh, theHIVE and clamAV
System will provide a victim node and a management node The system will deploy a lab environment to simulate an SOC lab setup, without students intervention for setup
Intermediate System shall ensure connectivity between tools and can monitor edge devices on the management node The system will forward logs from victim nodes to the management node to observe endpoint activities and detect threats through the centralized control
System will allow students to respond to incidents based on red teams attack Students can take action when threats are detected such as executing response measures based on alerts generated during a simulated attack from the red team
Students can open and create cases within case management platform Students can formally manage incidents by creating cases in theHIVE where they can document findings, track investigation progress and organize their response
Advanced System shall support full incident response workflow Enables students to follow a complete and structured incident response process, starting from threat detection in Wazuh, progressing to case creation and investigation in TheHive, and ending with appropriate response and recovery actions, that reflects real-world SOC practices
System shall be able to evaluate performance through metrics The system will collect and analyze logs, alerts, and case data to measure performance metrics
