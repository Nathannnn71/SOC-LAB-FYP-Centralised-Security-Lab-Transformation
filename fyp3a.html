PROBLEM STATEMENT
The lab currently has no deception technology. All attack detection is reactive — alerts fire after damage. There is no mechanism to collect intelligence about attacker behaviour, preferred tools, or entry techniques. Without honeypots, the lab cannot teach proactive threat intelligence or give students experience with realistic external attack data.
RESEARCH QUESTIONS
  1. Which honeypot platform (T-Pot, Cowrie, Dionaea) provides the richest attack telemetry with the lowest maintenance overhead for a university lab?
  2. How can honeypot data be correlated with SIEM events (FYP-2A) to produce actionable threat intelligence reports?
  3. What attack patterns emerge from lab Red Team exercises vs simulated external attacks over a full semester?
SCOPE & TASKS
  1. 01Select and deploy honeypot framework in VLAN 50 (isolated); configure Cowrie (SSH), Dionaea (malware), Honeytrap or T-Pot.
  2. 02Collect and structure attack data: attacker IPs, tools used, payloads, brute-force attempts, malware samples captured.
  3. 03Integrate honeypot event stream with FYP-2A SIEM for cross-source correlation.
  4. 04Build threat intelligence dashboard: most common attack vectors, trending techniques (MITRE ATT&CK mapping), geographic sources.
  5. 05Produce end-of-semester threat analysis report; recommend defensive posture improvements based on findings.
